# British Consolidation And Administrative Reform

### Assam History: Early British Relations
In 1823, **David Scott** was appointed the agent to the Governor General for the northeastern region.

*   **1824**: Treaty with **Ram Singh**, Raja of **Jaintia** – Raja owed allegiance to the British.
*   **1824**: Treaty of **Badarpur** – **Govinda Chandra**, Raja of **Cachar**, becomes a vassal of the British government.
*   **1826**: The **Khamtis** (under **Sadiya Khowa Gohain**) and **Moamarias** (under **Matibar Bar Senapati**) accepted British suzerainty.
*   **1826**: **Singpho** chiefs under **Beesa Gaum** acknowledged British supremacy.
*   **Gambhir Singh**: Was recognized as the ruler of **Manipur**.

### Proposals for Administrative Division
**David Scott** submitted a fresh proposal in 1828:
1.  Retention of lower Assam by the British up to **Biswanath**.
2.  Hand over of Upper Assam (from **Biswanath** to **Burhi Dihing**) to an Ahom prince.

The Company accepted the first proposal and, in 1828, annexed lower Assam up to **Biswanath**.

*   **David Scott**: Senior Commissioner, Lower Assam.
*   **Col. Richards**: Junior Commissioner, Upper Assam.

**1828**: Assam was made a non-regulated province. Territories were to be governed by an executive composed partly of civilians and partly of soldiers upon a mixed system into which the spirit of Regulations is infused in such a way as to cause it to harmonize and blend itself with all that is good in the spirit of the native institutions.

### Military Reforms
The regular army of the **EIC** (East India Company) left Assam after 1826. A new regiment, "**Assam Light Infantry**," was introduced in Assam with Hindustani, Gorkha, and some Manipuri and local soldiers. One troop was posted in **Biswanath** and the other troop was posted in **Sadiya**.

### Revenue Reforms of David Scott

#### Upper Assam
*   Instead of personal service, a tax of **Rs. 3** was collected from the *paiks*.
*   An Ahom noble named **Janardan Barbarua** was made the head of the revenue department in upper Assam.
*   He was assisted by officers like **Hazarika**, **Saikia**, **Bora**, etc.
*   Territorial *khels* were introduced and *kheldars* were given the responsibility of tax collection.
*   A district was divided into various *Mouzas* and each *mouza* was placed under an officer called **Mouzadar**.

#### Lower Assam
*   Poll tax (**Kharikatana**) was also collected from them, which is known as "**Paik tax**" in **Kamarup** and "**Hearth tax**" in **Darrang**.
*   **Proposals to increase the revenue**:
    *   Poll tax was extended to include even slaves.
    *   Withdrawal of **Narayani** currency and circulation of the company’s **Sikka** currency.
    *   To levy a tax on opium cultivation.

### Judicial Reforms of David Scott

#### Upper Assam
*   Civil cases were tried by the head of the revenue department and a coadjutor.
*   Minor cases were referred to the **panchayats**.
*   Criminal cases were usually disposed of by the junior commissioner or sometimes by the **Borphukan**.
*   Serious offences were tried by a jury presided over by the **Borphukan**, although the verdict was subject to revision by the commissioners.

#### Lower Assam
*   The senior commissioner tried civil cases without any limit and criminal cases not involving death sentences.
*   To reduce the congestion of the courts at the headquarters, **Scott** set up **moffusil panchayats**, comprising members elected by the people of the locality.
*   In 1828, **Scott's** proposal to constitute a **bar panchayat** with three Assamese judges assisted by two pundits and six assessors was approved by the government.

### First Revolt Against the British (1828)
*   **Leaders**: **Gomdhar Konwar** and **Dhanjay Bargohain**.
*   **Gomdhar** was formally enthroned near **Jorhat**.
*   By the end of November 1828, they proceeded towards **Mariani**.
*   They were intercepted by **Lt. Rutherford**.
*   **Gomdhar** fled to **Naga Hill**, later surrendered. He was sentenced to imprisonment without labour for seven years.
*   **Dhanjay** was sentenced to death but later escaped.

### Second Attempt
*   **Dhanjay** planned a second revolt with his sons **Harakanta** and **Haranath** and others—**Peali Barphukan** (son of **Badan Barphukan**), **Jeoram Dulia Barua**, etc.
*   The British arrested the leaders except **Dhanjay** who again fled. **Peali Phukan** and **Jeoram Dulia Barua** were hanged in 1830 at **Sibsagar** on the bank of the **Sibsagar Tank**.

### Revolt by the Khasis
*   British were making a road from **Assam** to **Sylhet** through **Khasi Hills**.
*   A bungalow and police outpost were erected at **Nongklao**.
*   **Siem** of **Nongklao**, **Teerut Singh**, with a band of about 500 Khasis attacked the British in 1829.
*   Two British officers were killed, **Lt. Burlton** and **Bedingsfield**, besides sixty Indian sepoys at **Nongkhlao**.
*   Under such circumstances, **Scott** again submitted a proposal for the restoration of Ahom monarchy in Upper Assam.
*   **Lord William Bentinck** agreed to **Scott's** proposal.
*   Of the several claimants to the throne, the choice fell ultimately on **Purandar Singha**.
*   **Teerut Singh** finally surrendered in 1833 and died in 1835.
*   On 2nd March 1833, a treaty was signed between the British and **Purandar Singha** known as the **Treaty of Guwahati**.

### Treaty of Guwahati (1833)
*   Portion of Assam lying east of the **Dhansiri** river on the south bank and east of **Biswanath** on the north bank of **Brahmaputra** was handed over to **Purandar Singha**.
*   **Purandar Singha** would pay **Rs. 50,000** to the company as annual tribute.
*   The Raja would abide by the principles of British jurisprudence and abstain from the earlier Ahom practice of cruel punishment.
*   Raja was assured of British protection on the condition of loyalty. If he was found to be unfaithful or guilty of oppressing his subjects, the Company's government reserved the right to either hand over the territory to another ruler or to annex it to the British dominions.

### British Consolidation and Further Developments
*   Soon, the British started regretting their decision of handing over upper Assam to the Ahoms.
*   Taking advantage of some internal strife in the **Muttock** kingdom, in December 1839, the entire **Muttock** territory was annexed by the British.
*   **Purandar Singha** started facing discrimination. **Charles Holroyd** (British officer) seized all the facilities provided to him due to a tea garden dispute.
*   The British came to know about the conspiracy. **Major Hannay** placed pickets at strategic locations.
*   There was an acute transport problem. The number of British officers in Assam was less. The old aristocracy was sure to join **Maniram**. The hill people, too, were sure to extend their support to the rebels.
*   In 1851-52, the British introduced **abkari** opium and started the opium trade in Assam. In 1860, poppy cultivation was banned in Assam. (**Nagaon** was the largest opium producing district in Assam).
*   Only after 1919 reforms, Assam was made a governor's province with its own provincial council.
*   All were suffering because of exploitative economic policies of the British.
*   In the meantime, the British held the first round table conference in London in 1930. It failed because the **INC** did not participate.
*   Following the third **RTC** (1932), the British Government drafted its proposals for the reforms in India and embodied them in a **White Paper** (1933). On the basis of this, a bill was prepared and passed as the **Govt. of India Act, 1935**.
*   A military train passing by derailed and many British and American soldiers lost their lives.
*   Accusing **Kushal Konwar** as the chief conspirator of the train sabotage, the British police arrested him.
*   The British suppressed the movement with an iron hand. The condition of the prisoners was distressing.

### Bengal Partition
*   Assam was made a part of the **Bengal Presidency** and a commissioner was appointed subordinate to the **Governor General of Bengal**.
*   In 1874, Assam was separated from the **Bengal Presidency**, and its status was upgraded to a **Chief Commissioner's province**.
*   In 1905, Bengal was partitioned and **East Bengal and Assam** was formed, governed by a **Lieutenant Governor**.
*   Following the annulment of the **Bengal partition** in 1911, Assam again became a **Chief Commissioner's province** with a Legislative Council of its own, at **Shillong**, from April 1, 1912. (First Council: 25 members: 14 nominated, 11 elected).
*   The restoration of the status quo was a welcome relief to the Assamese intelligentsia, but the leading intelligentsia of the **Surma valley** (especially **Sylhet**) considered that a deep injury and a deep wound had been inflicted on the Bengali-speaking population by tagging them to Assam.

### Chargola Tragedy / Chargola Exodus
On May 2, 1921, the labourers in thirteen tea gardens in the **Chargola** and **Longai** valley of **Karimganj** went on strike demanding a wage increase. This mass action of the labourers was termed officially "an **Exodus**". This sudden influx of thousands of labourers had almost paralysed the civil administration of **Karimganj**. On May 21, district authority decided to clear up the assembled labourers for the place. The armed police and the officials went into action at midnight, and in the clash many lost their lives.

### Land Policy and the Immigration Question

#### Administrative and Revenue Systems
*   The region was divided into various **parganas** under a **Choudhury**, assisted by a **Sirastadar**, **Tahbildar**, **Patowari**, **Thakuria**, etc.
*   A professional tax was levied on **Graziers**, **silk weavers**, **gold washers**, **fishermen**, **blacksmiths**, etc.
*   Land survey was completed under **Cpt. Mathews**.
*   Tax-free lands under **Ahoms** (**lakhiraj lands**) such as **Devottar**, **Dharmottar**, and **Brahmottar** were also brought under revenue collection.

#### Paik System and Land Allotment
*   Each **paik** was given 3 pura of land (**ga-mati**) and in return, Rs. 2 tax (**ga-dhan**) was collected from them.
*   Additional allotment: Each **paik** was allotted three puras of cultivable land lying fallow as **juma-mati** in addition to the **ga-mati**, at the rate of seven annas per pura.

#### Resettlement of Land
*   **Sir William Ward**, the Chief Commissioner of Assam, increased land revenue to 70–80% and in some cases even to 100%.

    *   Attended by **Gandhiji** (second visit), **Motilal Nehru**, **Vallabhbhai Patel**, **Rajendra Prasad**, **Madan Mohan Malviya**, **Sarojini Naidu**, etc.

#### Hindu Mahasabha
*   Besides the Congress session, the Annual Conference of the **Hindu Mahasabha** was held in Guwahati under the presidentship of **Madan Mohan Malaviya**.
*   The welcome address was presented by **Pitambardev Goswami**, head of the **Garmur Satra**.
*   The speech was tendered into English by **T.R. Phukan**.

#### Civil Disobedience Movement (CDM)
*   The **APCC**, under the presidentship of **Bisnuram Medhi**, launched the **CDM** in Assam.
*   **Liladhar Barua** of Assam joined the **Dandi March**.
*   **Cunningham Circular**: **Mr. J.A. Cunningham**, the then Director of Public Instruction of Assam, promulgated a circular to all academic heads of the province.

#### Women’s Participation
*   **Assam Mahila Samiti**: Formed by **Chandraprabha Saikiani** in 1926, enabling the women of Assam to get organized.
*   **Guneswari Devi** and **Mukteswari Devi** of Nogaon were the first ladies in the province to be imprisoned in the course of the national struggle.
*   **Chandraprabha Saikiani** was the leader of the women picketers at Gauhati.
*   **Girija Bala Gupta** was organizing the womenfolk at Karimganj.
*   **Gaidilieu** was captured, put in Imphal jail, and sentenced to transportation for life in January 1933.
*   From Assam, **Chandradhar Barua** attended the first **Round Table Conference**.

#### Spread of Socialist Ideas
*   Following the **Russian Revolution of 1917**, socialist and communist ideas influenced many young Congress leaders including **Nehru** and **Bose**.
*   **Jawaharlal Nehru** visited Assam in 1937.
*   **Subhas Chandra Bose** visited Assam in 1938 and 1939.
*   The **Assam Congress Socialist Party** was formed with **Sriman Prafulla Goswami** as the General Secretary.

#### Government of India Act, 1935 and Ministries
*   **Legislative Assembly**: 108 members (all elected).
*   **Legislative Council**: 22 members (18 elected).
*   General election was held in February 1937.
*   **T.R. Phukan** and **R.K. Choudhury** left Congress and formed a **United Peoples Party**.

**Ministries Chronology:**
*   **Sadullah Ministry**: 1 April 1937 – 18 September 1938.
*   **First Bordoloi Ministry**: 19 September 1938 – 16 November 1939.

**Government Formation Details:**
*   Congress secured 33 out of 108 seats.
*   Congress was not inclined to form a coalition government.
*   **Sir Muhammad Saadullah** (Assam Valley Muslim Party) formed the government on 1 April 1937; the governor at that time was **Sir Robert Reid**. Later, **Sir Saadullah** joined the **Muslim League**.
*   **Sir Saadullah** tendered his resignation and a new government was formed under **Gopinath Bordoloi**.
*   **Gopinath Bordoloi** assumed office on 19 September. It consisted of five members: **Kamini Kumar Chanda**, two SC members (**Ramnath Das** and **Akshay Kr. Das**), and one from the **Tribal League** (**Rupnath Brahma**).
*   Subsequently, three Muslim members were included: **Fakhruddin Ali Ahmed**, **Ali Hyder Khan**, and **Muhammad Ali**.
*   In 1939, Congress ministers all over India resigned.
*   **Saadullah ministry** emerged again with a commitment to war efforts.
*   **Gopinath Bordoloi**: 6 June 1890 – 5 August 1950.

#### Immigration Issue and Land Policy
*   Crisis of **Saadullah Ministry**.
*   Waste lands were classified into three categories.
*   No immigrant would be allowed to hold more than 16 bighas of land.
*   Local land-holders would be prohibited from subletting lands to immigrants and employing them as agricultural labour.
*   In 1941, the **Saadullah government** announced the **Land Development Scheme**. It was to open particular areas for settlement to the indigenous people, to immigrants arriving before 1938, and to people affected by flood and erosion and squatting illegally in the lined villages and grazing reserves.

# The Path To Independence And Political Integration

### Early British Administration and Resistance
*   The **Raja** agreed to abide by the advice of the **Political Agent, Upper Assam** and the **Agent to the Governor General, North East Frontier**.
*   He instructed **Adam White**, the political agent, to survey **Purandar Singha's** territory and report to him.
*   The **Khamtis** rebelled in 1839. Around 600 armed men attacked a cantonment and killed many including the political agent, **Adam White**.
*   The people of **Rangia** and **Lachima** in **Kamrup** and **Patharughat** in **Darrang** launched thereupon a no-tax campaign declaring excommunication to be the penalty for any one who disobeyed the **raij** (people).
*   **1894:** Patharughat.

### Political Associations and Student Activism
*   A circular required the students as well as their parents and guardians to give an undertaking in writing that the students would refrain from participation in any kind of political activities and would abide by the disciplinary rules and orders of the institution heads and the Education department.
*   The **Naga 'queen' Gaidinlieu** and her followers raised the banner of independence and called her people to rally around it in **Manipur**.
*   The **Cotton College** students protested against raising of war-funds by holding a science exhibition in their laboratory and went on strike on 6 December 1941.
*   The students went on a procession and **Mr. Humphrey**, the **D. C.**, ordered a lathi charge on the striking students.
*   This created great indignation towards the **Saadullah** ministry, and the country-wide reaction to this incident precipitated its fall.
*   **AISF** also observed a **'Gauhati Day'** to express their solidarity with the students in Assam.
*   In view of these developments, **Saadullah** submitted his resignation on 12 December 1941. The Governor, **Robert Reid**, took over the administration of the province on 25 December 1941.

### The Quit India Movement (1942)
*   In 1942, the **Cripps Mission** failed.
*   **INC** took up the **"Quit India" Resolution** on 8th August 1942.
*   **Gandhi** gave the slogan **"Do or Die"**.
*   Within a week of the declaration of the 'Quit India' Resolution, all important **Congress** leaders were arrested.
*   **Gopinath Bordoloi** and **Siddhi Nath Sarma** were arrested at **Dhubri** while returning from attending the **AICC** meeting.
*   **Pitambar Dev Goswami** (**Satradhikar Gormur Satra**) and his deputy, **Krishna Dev Goswami**, were detained.
*   The people of Assam defied the creed of non-violence by taking to sabotage activities.

### Martyrs and Resistance
#### Gohpur Police Station
*   **Kanaklata Baruah** led a procession of unarmed villagers in the revolution camp of **Gohpur** division of undivided **Darrang** district to hoist and unfurl the national flag at the **Gohpur** police station.
*   Consequently, the police opened fire and she was shot dead holding the flag, which was subsequently picked up by her compatriot **Mukunda Kakati**, who was also killed later.
*   That is why she is also known as **"Birbala"**.

#### Swahid Kushal Konwar
*   On 10 October 1942, hidden in the thick fog of early morning, some people removed few sleepers of the railway line near **Sarupathar** in **Golaghat** district.
*   An ardent follower of **Gandhiji** and his principle of nonviolence, **Kushal** was ignorant about the sabotage plan and action.
*   At dawn on 15 June 1943 at 4:30 am, **Kushal Konwar** was hanged in **Jorhat Jail**.

#### Other Women Martyrs
*   **Bhogeswari Phukanani**, Nagaon
*   **Tileswari Barua**, Dhekiajuli
*   **Rebati Lahon**, Teok
*   **Khahuli Devi**, Dhekiajuli
*   **Kumali Devi**, Dhekiajuli

#### Underground Activity and Sacrifices
*   Local cells like **Ahimsa Biplavi Sangha**, **Hanuman Party**, **Karma Parisad**, **Mrityu Bahini**, etc., engaged themselves in various kinds of underground activity.
*   **Kamala Miri**, a detenue in the **Jorhat** jail, died on 22 April 1943 due to lack of treatment.

### The Line System and Immigration
*   Towards the end of 1920s, the government introduced a colonization scheme by which the immigrants were disallowed to settle beyond certain compact areas earmarked to that end.
*   Extended land holding from 16 to 20 bigha on payment of a premium.
*   **The Line System** was introduced in 1920.

| Category | Land Designation |
| :--- | :--- |
| Local People | Available exclusively to local people |
| Immigrants | Available exclusively to immigrants |
| Both | Available to both |

*   **C. S. Mullan**, Census Commissioner, observed in his **Census Report of 1931**: ".... By 1921 the first army crops of the invaders had conquered **Goalpara**. The second army crops which followed them in the years 1921-31 completed the conquest of **Nogaon**. The **Barpeta** sub-division of **Kamrup** has also fallen to their attack and **Darrang** is being invaded...... **Sibsagar** would ultimately remain the only district, where an Assamese race would find a home of its own."
*   It was recognized as an unmistakable part of a grand conspiracy to include **Assam** in **Pakistan**.

### Political Developments (1946–1947)
*   Election was held in 1946.
*   **Congress** secured a majority and **Gopinath Bordoloi** formed the second Ministry on 11 February 1946.
*   **Cabinet Mission, 1946**:
    *   It rejected the demand of **Pakistan** outrightly.
    *   It divided the provinces in three groupings:
        *   **Sec A**: Hindu majority provinces.
        *   **Sec B**: Muslim majority provinces.
        *   The princely states could join either group A, B, C or join **Britain**.
    *   The grouping plan created wide resentment in **Assam**.
    *   **Asam Jatiya Mahasabha** collected some secret papers showing the designs of the **League** in **Assam**.
    *   Hundreds of protest meetings were held all over **Assam**.

### Mountbatten Plan
*   India will be set independent on 15th August 1947.
*   Referendum will be conducted in **Sindh**, **NWFP**, **Sylhet**. If they vote yes then they will join **Pakistan**.
*   Princely states will either join **India** or **Pakistan**.
