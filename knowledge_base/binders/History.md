# Subject: History

## Topic: ASSAM HISTORY
### ASSAM HISTORY

*   **1823**: David Scott appointed Agent to the Governor-General for the Northeast.
*   **1824**:
    *   Treaty with Ram Singh, Raja of Jaintia (British allegiance).
    *   Treaty of Badarpur: Govinda Chandra, Raja of Cachar, becomes a British vassal.
*   **1826**:
    *   Khamtis (Sadiya Khowa Gohain) & Moamarias (Matibar Bar Senapati) accept British suzerainty.
    *   Singpho chiefs under Beesa Gaum acknowledge British supremacy.
*   **1828**:
    *   David Scott's proposal: Retain Lower Assam (to Biswanath); hand Upper Assam (Biswanath → Burhi Dihing) to an Ahom prince.
    *   Company accepts; annexes Lower Assam up to Biswanath.
    *   David Scott: Senior Commissioner, Lower Assam; Col Richards: Junior Commissioner, Upper Assam.
    *   Assam becomes a **non-regulated province** (mixed civil-military administration).

#### Military reforms
*   EIC regular army leaves (post-1826).
*   New **Assam Light Infantry** raised (Hindustani, Gorkha, Manipuri, local troops).
*   Two troops stationed: Biswanath & Sadiya.

#### Revenue reforms (David Scott)

| Region      | Key Features                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| :---------- | :--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Upper Assam | • Tax of Rs 3 per paik (personal service abolished).<br>• Janardan Barbarua (Ahom noble) heads revenue; assisted by Hazarika, Saikia, Bora, etc.<br>• Territorial **khels**; **kheldars** collect tax.<br>• Administrative hierarchy: Districts → Mouzas → Mouzadar.                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| Lower Assam | • Divided into **parganas** under a **Choudhury** (assisted by Sirastadar, Tahbildar, Patowari, Thakuria).<br>• Professional tax on graziers, silk-weavers, gold-washers, fishermen, blacksmiths.<br>• Land survey by Capt. Mathews.<br>• Tax-free Ahom lands (Devottar, Dharmottar, Brahmottar) incorporated into revenue system.<br>• Each paik: 3 pura land → Rs 2 tax (ga-dhan).<br>• Poll tax (Kharikatana) – “Paik tax” (Kamarup) / “Hearth tax” (Darrang). |
| **Proposals to increase revenue** | 1. Allocate three additional fallow *juma-mati* puras at 7 annas each.<br>2. Extend poll tax to slaves.<br>3. Replace Narayani currency with Company **sikka**.<br>4. Tax opium cultivation. |

#### Judicial reforms (David Scott)

| Region          | Civil Cases                                                     | Criminal Cases                                                                      |
| :-------------- | :-------------------------------------------------------------- | :---------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Upper Assam** | Tried by revenue head + co-adjutor; minor cases → panchayats.   | Junior commissioner or Borphukan; serious offences → jury presided by Borphukan (subject to commissioner revision). |
| **Lower Assam** | Senior commissioner handles all civil cases; criminal cases **except death-sentences**. | Established **moffusil panchayats** (elected locals).<br>1828: Bar-panchayat approved – 3 Assamese judges, 2 pundits, 6 assessors. |

#### Revolts & Resistance
*   **First Revolt (1828)**:
    *   Leaders: Gomdhar Konwar, Dhanjay Bargohain.
    *   Gomdhar enthroned near Jorhat, marched to Mariani, intercepted by Lt. Rutherford.
    *   Outcome: Gomdhar imprisoned 7 years; Dhanjay sentenced to death, escaped.
*   **Second Attempt (1830)**:
    *   Leaders: Dhanjay, sons Harakanta & Haranath, Peali Barphukan, Jeoram Dulia Barua.
    *   Leaders arrested; Peali Phukan & Jeoram hanged at Sibsagar (1830).
*   **Khasis Revolt (1829)**:
    *   Siem of Nongkhlao & ~500 Khasis attack road-construction party.
    *   Lt. Burlton & Bedingsfield killed, 60 sepoys dead.

#### Restoration of Ahom Monarchy (Upper Assam)
*   Scott proposes restoration; **Lord William Bentick** approves.
*   **Purandar Singha** selected; David Scott dies (1831).
*   **Treaty of Guwahati (2 Mar 1833)**:
    *   Territory east of Dhansiri (south bank) & east of Biswanath (north bank) handed to Purandar Singha.
    *   Annual tribute **Rs 50,000**.
    *   Raja to follow British jurisprudence, obey Political Agent, release fugitives, abstain from cruelty, ban **Sati**, cooperate on opium cultivation, accept British protection (with right of annexation on disloyalty).

#### Challenges to Purandar Singha
*   Noble resentment, kheldar qualifications, land resettlement, Bengal connections, financial strain, migration, coin shortage.

#### Annexation of Upper Assam (1838)
*   British discover tea (Robert Bruce, 1823) → Assam Tea Co. (1839); coal & petroleum potential.
*   Complaints against Purandar; **Capt. F. Jenkins** (Commissioner, 1834) commissions survey (Adam White).
*   Unsatisfactory report → Upper Assam annexed (1838).

#### Timeline of Subsequent Annexations

*   **1830**:
    *   *Sadiya*: Singpho revolt begins.
    *   *Cachar*: Govinda Chandra assassinated.
*   **1832**:
    *   *Cachar*: Annexed by the British.
    *   *Jayantia*: Ram Singh dies; Rajendra Singh succeeds.
*   **1835**:
    *   *Jayantia*: Formal annexation.
*   **1839**:
    *   *Sadiya*: Singpho revolt (1830) continues; Khamti revolt culminates with the killing of Adam White.
    *   *Muttock*: Internal strife exploited; full annexation completed in December 1839.
*   **1854**:
    *   *Cachar*: North Cachar annexed after suppressing the Tularam Senapati revolt.

#### Revolt of 1857 (Assam)
*   Leader: **Maniram Dewan** (“Kalita Raja”), former tehsildar/sheristadar, Prime Minister under Purandar Singha, tea pioneer at Cinnamara.
*   Planned restoration of Ahom rule under **Kandarpeswar Singha** (Purandar’s grandson).

#### Agrarian & Peasant Uprisings
*   **Phulaguri Uprising (1861)**:
    *   1,500 ryots protest opium tax.
    *   Lt. Singer killed; violent suppression.
*   **Assam Ryots (1893-94)**:
    *   Land revenue hikes (70-100%).
    *   No-tax campaigns in Rangia, Lachima, Patharughat.
    *   Police firing on protesters (Krishak Swahid Divas).

#### Language & Literary Developments
*   **Bengali official language** (1836-1873) → “Dark Age” for Assamese.
*   Early Assamese publications:
    *   **Bible** (1813, Carey & Marshman).
    *   **Orunodoi** (first Assamese monthly, 1846, Baptist press).
    *   **A Grammar of the Assamese Language** (W. Robinson, 1839).
*   Literary societies & periodicals:
    *   **Gyan Pradayini Sabha** (1857), **Asamiya Sahitya Sabha** (1872), **Asam Desh Hitaisini Sabha** (1885), **Asamiya Bhaxar Unnati Sadhini Sabha** (1888) → launched **Jonaki** (1889).
    *   Notable works: “Bon Kunwori” (Chandra Kumar Agarwala), “Priyotomar Sithi” (Hemchandra Goswami), humorous column by Lakshminath Bezbaroa.

#### Political Organizations
*   **Jorhat Sarvajanik Sabha** (1884), **Tezpur Ryot Sabha** (1884), **Assam Association** (1903, Guwahati; defunct 1921).
*   **Student bodies**: Assam Chatra Sanmilan (1916, first president L. Bezbaroa), Surma Valley Students’ Federation.

#### Administrative Changes (Bengal Presidency)

| Period        | Status                     | Key Commissioners                                           |
| :------------ | :------------------------- | :---------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1828-1874     | Part of Bengal Presidency  | D. Scott (1828-31), T. C. Robertson (1831-34), F. Jenkins (1834-61), H. Hopkinson (1861-74) |
| 1874-1905     | Chief-Commissioner’s Province | R. H. Keatinge, Sir H. Cotton (1896-02), Sir J. B. Fuller       |
| 1905-1911     | East Bengal & Assam (Lt. Governor) | Sir J. B. Fuller, C. S. Bayley                              |

> ⚡ **Key Revision Fact**: The 1905 Partition created *East Bengal and Assam*; its reversal in 1911 restored Assam as a Chief-Commissioner’s province with its own Legislative Council (1912) and later a Governor’s province (1919).

#### Swadeshi & Extremist Phase
*   Extremist youths (Ambikagiri Raychoudhury, Raktim Bora, Triguna Barua) formed **Seva Sanga** (later militant).
*   Raychoudhury wrote drama *Bandini Bharat*; under police watch until 1915.
*   Assam Association’s 1907 memorandum ignored.

#### Non-Cooperation Movement (1920-22)
*   Inspired by Jallianwala Bagh, Khilafat, and Gandhi’s call.
*   **INC (Nagpur) resolution (Sept 1920)** → Assam Association supports NCM.
*   **Gandhi visit (Aug 1921)**; praised Assam’s progress; established **Assam Pradesh Congress Committee (APCC)** (5 Jun 1921, N. C. Bordoloi’s residence).
    *   President: Krishnakanta Bhattacharya.
    *   Exec: Kuladhar Chaliha (Pres.), Badrinarayan Agarwala (VP), N. C. Bordoloi (GS).
    *   Later presidents: T. R. Phukan, etc.

#### Chargola Tragedy (May 1921)
*   9,000 tea-garden labourers strike for wage rise; attempt to cross Padma; mass “Exodus”.
*   Police crackdown (21 May) → casualties.

#### Women’s Participation
*   Pioneers: Nalinibala Devi, Puspalata Das, Chandraprabha Saikiani, Girija Devi, Bidyutprabha Devi.
*   **Mongri (Malati Maam)** – first Assamese woman killed in NCM.
*   **Bhanumati Talukdar** – “Sarojini of Assam”.
*   **Pramila Medak** – continued activism despite excommunication.

#### Post-NCM Political Shifts
*   **Swarajist Party** formed (1 Jan 1923) by C. R. Das & Motilal Nehru; Assam branch (Jul 1923) led by T. R. Phukan.
*   **Asamiya Samrakshini Sabha** (Ambikagiri Roy Choudhury) → later **Assam Jatiya Mahasabha** (cultural defence, “dual citizenship”).
*   **Assam Mahila Samiti** (Chandraprabha Saikiani, 1926) → first organized women’s body; early female political prisoners: Guneswari Devi, Mukteswari Devi.

#### National & International Engagement
*   **Round Table Conference (1930)** – Chandradhar Barua represented Assam (first conference).
*   **Saadullah Ministry crisis (Dec 1941)**:
    *   Cotton College students strike; lathi charge.
    *   Ministry resigns (12 Dec 1941).
    *   Governor Robert Reid assumes control (25 Dec 1941).

### LAND REVENUE
LAND REVENUE

Revenue assessed on the land, or any tax imposed in liue of revenue assessable on the land is called land- revenue.The Hoe-tax or house tax is an annual tax levied on a family or house of persons or on each male persons completing the age of 18yrs and taking part in cultivation.

The Amendment Act 2024 inserted section 28 A as follows: 

Any specified area where substantial agricultural activities ha Rightsve ceased and is not a town land, the state Govt may at any time declare such land as town land by notification published in the official Gazette for the purpose of this Regulation.

Rights of land holders

A land holder has a permanent, heritable and transferable right to use and occupancy in his estates but he is entitled to re-settlement of the land from the Govt and Govt may enhance the revenue.

THE ASSAM TEMPORARILY SETTELED AREAS TENANCY ACT 1971

This Act regulate the relations of land lord and tenant in temporarily settled areas of Assam. In this Act land means Agricultural lands which is used for agricultural purposes.

Agriculture includes horticulture, pisciculture and other allied agriculture pursuits.

Landlord means a person immediately under whom a tenant holds but does not include any Govt.

Rent means whatever is lawful payable or deliverable by the tenant to the landlord in cash or in kind.

Revenue officer means any officer with that designation appointed by the State Government for the purpose of this court.

Revenue Court means the Court of Deputy Commissioner, settlement officer or of such other officer as the State Govt. may invest with the powers of Revenue Court for the purpose of this Act.

Tenants are classified into occupancy Tenant and Non- occupancy Tenant.

Occupancy tenant: Who held the land continuously for 3 yrs have a right to occupancy on that land.

Non-occupancy tenant: A tenant holding immediately under a proprietor, land holder or settlement holder other than land holder but not having a right of occupancy in the land held by him.

ACQUISITION OF OWNERSHIP RIGHTS AND INTERMEDIARY RIGHTS BY GOVT.FOR TENANTS.

The State Govt. may at any time after the commencement of this Act, by notification declare that the rights/ titles and interests of any or all land -lords in respect of holding held and personally cultivated by the occupancy tenants , shall transferred and vest in the respective occupancy tenants free from all encumbrances created by the land lord.

Compensation: The total compensation payable for acquiring the ownership rights as well as the intermediary rights if any, of any holding shall be an amount equal to 50 times the full rate of annual land revenue payable for such land.

PLAINTS: A Plaint is a statement of the claim. Its object is to simply state the grounds upon and the relief in respect of which the plaintiff seeks the assistance order of the court. Three essentials part of the plaint are as follows:

Heading and title: Name of the court should be written in the head of the plaint.

The Body of the plaint: It contains the statement of the plaint. ( Formal & Substantial)

Relief Claimed: relief must be claimed from the order of the court.

Written STATEMENT: A written statement may contain more or all of the several pleas such as , (a) Admissions and denials, (b) dilatory pleas, (c) Objection in point of Law, (d) Special defence, (e) Set off.

The Constitution of India empowers  the Supreme Court to issue Writs under Art 32 while empowers the High Court Under Art 226.

Writ of Habeas Corpus: Is the nature of an order calling upon the person who has detained another to produce the latter before the court to let the court know on what ground he has been confined and set him free if there  is no legal justification for the imprisonment.

PROCEDURE FOR PAYMENT OF COMPENSATION

DC shall give notice to the land lord and all other persons having interests in the land.

Fix a copy thereof in a conspicuous place in his office.

Make an enquiry, hear objections if any

Determine the compensation payable.

RENT: Liability to pay rent either agreement or customer, a tenant shall be liable to pay rent whether in cash or kind at a rate maximum rate of rent not exceeding 3 times of land revenue payable for such land.

Rent suit: No arrear of rent shall be realisable otherwise than by a rent suit filed in the competent civil court according to the provisions of Civil Procedure Code 1908.

EJECTMENT: If a tenant sub-lets or transfers the whole or any part of his holding otherwise than in accordance with the provisions of this Act, then the tenant’s interest thereon shall be forfeited.

No tenant shall be ejected from his holding except in execution of an ejectment decree passed by the competent civil court.

CODE OF CIVIL PROCEDURE 1908

The code of CPC amended several times and recent significant amendment (Karnataka Amendment) Act 2024 came into force on June 5 2025aims to expediate case disposal within 24 months from the date of first hearing.

Some important definitions

CODE: Includes rules.

 Decree: Decree means the final adjudication which, so far as regards the court expressing it, conclusively determines the rights of the parties with regard to all or any of the matters in controversy in the suit and may be either preliminary or final.

Decree-holder: Means any person in whose favour a decree or an order enforceable has been passed.

Judge: means the presiding officer of a civil court.

Order: means the decision pronounced by the civil court which is not a Decree.

Affidavit: is a document containing a declaration of facts made in writing and sworn before an officer having authority to administer oath.

Appeal: An Appeal is a judicial examination of the inferior court by a Higher Court and made on the basis of complaint by the appellant stating the decree passed by the lower court is erroneous.

Plaint: Plaint is a document containing the case and a statement of claim of the plaintiff. The plaintiff files a suit in a court and seeks relief.

Plaintiff: Is a person who initiates the suit for commencing an action against the defendant.

Suit: The term Suit generally means a civil proceeding which is commenced by presenting a plaint.

Defendant: A person who defends his case in a court of Law with regard to the suit instituted by the plaintiff against him.

Jurisdiction: Means the authority of the court or any other public authority vested with power to decide the case or issues.

Summons: Very important document in the litigation issued from the office of a court of justice that calls a person to attend the court before a judge or any other person presiding the court for hearing.

Written Statement: is a reply of defendant of the defence taken by him in regard to the plaint filed by the plaintiff.

Original and appellate Jurisdiction: in its original jurisdiction, a court entertains and adjudicates suits whereas under its appellate jurisdiction a court decides appeals.

Pecuniary jurisdiction: this term connotes the value of subject matter of suits. High Courts and District courts have no pecuniary limitation but the other courts have no such unlimited pecuniary jurisdiction.

 Resjudicata: S 11 of the code means that an issue or a point decided and attaining finally should not be allowed to be re-opened and reagitated twice over.
