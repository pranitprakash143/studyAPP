## Summary
Early immigration policies in British India, particularly in Assam, involved schemes like the colonization scheme (late 1920s) and the Line System (1920) to regulate immigrant settlement and land ownership. Political developments leading up to India's independence, including the Cabinet Mission and Mountbatten Plan, also significantly impacted Assam's demographic and political landscape due to concerns over immigration and potential inclusion in Pakistan.

## Key Facts
- A colonization scheme was introduced towards the end of the 1920s, disallowing immigrants from settling beyond earmarked compact areas.
- Under the colonization scheme, land holding was extended from 16 to 20 bigha on payment of a premium.
- The Line System, introduced in 1920, classified waste lands into three categories: exclusively for local people, exclusively for immigrants, and available to both.
- Under the Line System, no immigrant was allowed to hold more than 16 bighas of land.
- Local land-holders were prohibited from subletting lands to immigrants or employing them as agricultural labour under the Line System.
- C. S. Mullan, Census Commissioner, observed in his 1931 Census Report that 'invaders' had conquered Goalpara by 1921 and Nogaon by 1931, with Barpeta sub-division also fallen and Darrang being invaded.
- The Land Development Scheme was announced in 1941 by the Saadullah government.
- The 1941 Land Development Scheme aimed to open particular areas for settlement to indigenous people, immigrants arriving before 1938, and people affected by flood/erosion squatting illegally.
- The Land Development Scheme was recognized as part of a conspiracy to include Assam in Pakistan.
- In the 1946 election, Congress secured a majority, and Gopinath Bordoloi formed the second Ministry on February 11, 1946.
- The Cabinet Mission of 1946 rejected the demand for Pakistan outrightly.
- The Cabinet Mission divided provinces into three groupings: Sec A (Hindu majority), Sec B (Muslim majority), and Sec C (Assam and Bengal).
- The Cabinet Mission's grouping plan created wide resentment in Assam, leading to hundreds of protest meetings and support from Congress socialists and Communists for the anti-grouping agitation.
- The Mountbatten Plan declared India would be independent on August 15, 1947.
- Under the Mountbatten Plan, Hindu and Muslim members of legislative assemblies of Bengal and Punjab would vote separately on partition, and referendums would be conducted in Sindh, NWFP, and Sylhet to decide joining Pakistan.

## Quick Revision
- Late 1920s saw a colonization scheme restricting immigrant settlement to compact areas.
- The Line System (1920) classified lands for locals, immigrants, or both, limiting immigrant land to 16 bighas.
- C. S. Mullan's 1931 Census Report noted significant immigrant 'conquest' of Assam districts.
- The 1941 Land Development Scheme by Saadullah government was seen as a conspiracy to include Assam in Pakistan.
- The 1946 Cabinet Mission rejected Pakistan but proposed grouping Assam with Bengal (Sec C), causing widespread anti-grouping agitation.
