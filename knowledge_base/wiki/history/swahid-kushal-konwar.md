## Summary
Kushal Konwar was accused by the British police as the chief conspirator of the Sarupathar train sabotage on October 10, 1942, despite being an ardent follower of Gandhiji's nonviolence. He was subsequently hanged in Jorhat Jail on June 15, 1943. The incident involved the derailment of a military train, resulting in the loss of British and American soldiers' lives.

## Key Facts
- The Sarupathar train sabotage occurred on October 10, 1942, near Sarupathar in Golaghat district.
- The sabotage involved removing railway sleepers, leading to the derailment of a military train.
- The derailment resulted in the loss of lives of many British and American soldiers.
- Kushal Konwar was accused by the British police as the chief conspirator of the train sabotage.
- He was an ardent follower of Gandhiji's principle of nonviolence and was ignorant about the sabotage plan.
- Kushal Konwar was executed by hanging on June 15, 1943, at 4:30 am.
- His execution took place in Jorhat Jail.
- The British suppressed the movement with an iron hand, leading to distressing prisoner conditions.
- Kamala Miri, a detenue, died in Jorhat jail on April 22, 1943, due to lack of treatment.
- Other women participants included Bhogeswari Phukanani, Tileswari Barua, Rebati Lahon, Khahuli Devi, and Kumali Devi.
- Local underground cells like Ahimsa Biplavi Sangha, Hanuman Party, Karma Parisad, and Mrityu Bahini engaged in various activities.
